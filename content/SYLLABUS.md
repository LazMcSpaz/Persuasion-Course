# The syllabus

A skill ladder for a learner new to both NLP and hypnosis. The Milton
Model is the spine; the Meta Model is one compact module. Every module
builds on the ones before it and may use their techniques in its drills,
but teaches only its own.

The learner uses this in face-to-face sales, on phone and video calls, and
in everyday persuasion. The goal is mastery by repetition: assignments
outnumber lessons, and role-plays are run in a Claude chat from the
scenario cards.

Cut from the course, not taught anywhere: eye-accessing cues, preferred
representational systems and predicate matching, NLP anchoring of states,
submodalities, covert trance inductions, nested loops, reading lies from
the eyes.

## Part 1: Foundations

**m00 How to Practise.** The loop: learn, drill, role-play, field, log.
Why reps beat reading. The trust ledger: every debrief asks whether it
worked and whether they would be glad tomorrow that they talked to you.
How to keep the field log. Recording yourself. Short module: 1 or 2
lessons, a few drills about the method, assignments that set up the habit
(record a baseline conversation, first log entry).

**m01 Rapport.** Matching voice tempo, volume and energy; posture and
gesture matched loosely and late, never mirrored on the spot; on the phone
it is all voice. Backtracking: giving their key words back exactly. Naming
the emotion you hear. Evidence: Chartrand & Bargh, van Baaren, Maddux,
Lieberman.

**m02 Calibration.** Learning one person's baseline and noticing change
from it, not decoding body language from a chart. Their yes and no
signals, interest and doubt. What it cannot do (Bond & DePaulo). Phone
calibration: pace, pauses, breath, word choice.

**m03 Pacing and Leading.** Truisms: statements they can verify. Pace
several, then lead one. The yes set (unproven). Pacing their objections
before leading past them. Pacing what they feel, think or want, not only
what they see.

## Part 2: The Milton Model

**m04 Artfully Vague Language.** Nominalizations, unspecified verbs,
unspecified referential index ("people", "some", "they say"), simple and
comparative deletions ("better", "more"). Why vague language lets the
listener supply a meaning that fits them (Forer). When to be vague and
when to be exact (price, terms and facts are always exact).

**m05 Links, Causes and Readings.** Conjunctions ("and"), implied
causatives ("as", "while", "when", "during", "before"), cause and effect
("makes", "causes", "because"; Langer), complex equivalence ("which means"),
mind-reading ("you might be wondering"), lost performatives ("it's good to
take your time"), universal quantifiers ("everyone", "always").

**m06 NUVI and the Run-On Sentence.** Mike Mandel's NUVI: Nominalization
Unspecified Verb Integration. Talking without resolving: chaining
truisms, nominalizations and unspecified verbs with linkages ("and as",
"while", "because", "although", "and when", "and if", "and yet"), pausing
in the right places but never ending the sentence. The stated purpose: so
much language to process that the listener goes inward ("downtime") and
the critical mind stops checking. The evidence for the weaker version:
distraction stops counter-arguing (Petty, Wells & Brock), load makes
people believe first (Gilbert), confusion then a clear frame
(disrupt-then-reframe). And the warning in the same study: overload
helps a weak case and hurts a strong one, so do not bury a good argument.
Fluency is the skill: this module is mostly drills of talking for 30, 60,
then 120 seconds without stopping. Credit Mandel by name; describe the
method in our own words.

**m07 Presuppositions and Choices.** Time ("before you decide"),
ordinals ("the first thing you'll notice"), "or" (choice between two
yeses), awareness predicates ("you may already realise"), adverbs and
adjectives ("how easily"), commentary ("fortunately"). Illusion of choice
and double binds (unproven). Evidence: Loftus, Loftus & Palmer.

**m08 Embedded Commands and Questions.** Embedded commands and analogue
marking (tone drop, pause, glance): unproven, taught with a test plan.
Embedded questions ("I'm curious what you'd want from it"). Negative
commands ("don't decide yet") and Wegner. Conversational postulates ("can
you picture that?"). Tag questions ("isn't it?"). Quotes: putting a line in
someone else's mouth ("my mentor used to say, 'just try it'").

**m09 Stories and Metaphor.** Teaching tales in the manner of Erickson;
stories from other clients; metaphors that carry a point past resistance;
the one person, not the statistic. Evidence: Green & Brock, Small and
colleagues.

**m10 Utilization.** Erickson's principle: use whatever the person brings,
including resistance. "That's right, and…"; agreeing with the objection
and leading from inside it; permissive language and "but you're free"
(Carpenter); reactance (Brehm). Indirect versus direct (Lynn reviews).

## Part 3: The Toolkit Around It

**m11 Imagery and Future Pacing.** Getting them to picture owning, using,
having solved it; sensory detail (all senses, without typing people);
future pacing an objection away; Gregory, Cialdini & Carpenter.

**m12 Framing and Reframing.** Gain and loss frames (Tversky & Kahneman
1981), price anchors (1974), context reframes ("in what situation would
this be useful?"), content reframes ("what else could this mean?"),
reframing objections, disrupt-then-reframe revisited.

**m13 Precision Questions.** The Meta Model, compact: recovering
deletions ("compared to what?"), unspecified nouns and verbs ("who
specifically?", "how specifically?"), universals ("always?"), modal
operators ("what stops you?", "what would happen if you did?"). Use them in
discovery to surface the real need, and the real objection. Rackham.
The Milton Model is the Meta Model run backwards; show the pairing.

**m14 Small Yeses and Commitment.** Foot-in-the-door (Freedman & Fraser),
compliance sets, micro-agreements through a call, the ask, and the trust
cost of a commitment extracted rather than given.

## Part 4: Putting It Together

**m15 The Whole Conversation.** One conversation from opening to ask:
rapport and calibration, discovery with precision questions, presenting
with Milton language and imagery, handling objections with pacing,
utilization and reframes, and the ask. Separate run-throughs for face to
face, phone or video, and an everyday persuasion. Capstone: a field
project of ten logged conversations reviewed with Claude.
